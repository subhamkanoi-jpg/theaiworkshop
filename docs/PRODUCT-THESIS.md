# Product thesis

*The AI Workshop is not a class. It is not a newsletter. It is not a WhatsApp group with a logo. It is Kolkata’s applied-AI store: one door, a visible path, a thing you can buy this month, and a room you return to.*

---

## 1. What I found in the repo

The live site at theaiworkshop.in is a **single-SKU ticket booth**.

It does three things well:

- A brand voice that already sounds like a place (`AI is for the ones who show up.`).
- Real proof: Meetup #1 photos, 60+ people, Salt Lake, hosts’ faces.
- A clean booking machine: Razorpay, venue hold, 25-seat cap, `/book` as the ad landing page.

It fails the founder’s actual intention.

The intention, in the founder’s words: *a community where people who are genuinely interested in AI assemble, learn from each other, grow, and be part of the change.*

What the site currently sells: a 2-hour video-editing class on 30 August for ₹599.

Those are not the same product.

### The oscillation (read this before changing another headline)

Git history is a confession:

1. **Community club** — apply for membership, velvet rope, cohort scarcity, “what members get.”
2. **Practitioner network** — Become a Host, weekend slots, people teaching their own use case.
3. **Ticket booth** — 15 August 2026: membership was stripped so every CTA said “Reserve your seat.” Workshop as the only conversion path.

Each version was half-right.

- Membership without a thing to *do* is a vanity club.
- Hosts without a full room is a ghost town.
- Tickets without a path is a flyer that dies on Monday.

The synthesis is the mega store.

### Dead code that still knows the truth

These components exist, unwired:

- `ApplicationForm` — people wanted in.
- `BecomeHost` — practitioners wanted to teach.
- `InterestForm` — people wanted the next one, not necessarily *this* one.
- Portfolio screenshots — Meetup #1 actually shipped websites.
- Host photos (Yogesh, Neeraj, Subham) — used as anonymous avatars, never named on the homepage.

The product already tried to be a store, a club, and a faculty. It just never put them on one floor.

### What the current homepage hides

A first-time visitor cannot answer, in ten seconds:

1. What *is* this, if I am not already in the WhatsApp group?
2. What will I be able to do after a year here?
3. Who runs it?
4. What happened in June, and what happens after August?
5. Is this for a shop owner, a student, a job-seeker, or all three?

The poetry is strong. The wayfinding is not. IKEA never makes you guess which aisle is kitchens.

## 2. The product, said in one sentence

**The AI Workshop is an offline store in Kolkata where a beginner walks in, sees the whole path, buys this month’s skill, builds a real thing before they leave, and comes back.**

The ticket is the SKU. The path is the catalog. The room is the membership. The website is the storefront. WhatsApp is the receipt and the aisle you walk after closing time.

---

## 3. How the giants would build this

Not as decoration. As operating principles.

### Meta (the graph)

Zuckerberg’s actual product is not an app. It is a **graph with a ritual**.

- People need a reason to assemble in the same place at the same time.
- They need a thing they can show (`I made this`) so the graph grows without ads.
- They need a daily surface or the monthly event is a ghost.

Applied here:

| Meta primitive | Our primitive |
|---|---|
| Profile | The artifact you walk out with (site, reel, Gem) |
| Feed | WhatsApp: members posting what they shipped |
| Group | The room in Salt Lake, once a month |
| Event | The Sunday workshop |
| Creator | Table captains / hosts — alumni who run a table of 8–10 |
| Daily active | The “second you” you open on your phone on a Tuesday |

The 25-person cap is a beautiful salon. A salon does not become a movement. **A movement needs a 100-person ritual once, then small rooms forever.** 27 September is that ritual.

The flywheel, which is the whole company:

```
Show up
  → build a named artifact
    → post it (Instagram / WhatsApp status / LinkedIn)
      → a friend asks “where did you learn that?”
        → they book the next Sunday
          → alumni become table captains
            → 25 becomes 100 becomes 400
              → the room is the default place Kolkata goes to learn applied AI
```

If a workshop does not produce a **named, screenshot-able artifact**, it does not turn the flywheel. Notes do not turn it. Certificates do not turn it. A working thing does.

### Google (the default)

Pichai’s actual product is not Search. It is **being the default place you go when you have a question.**

Applied here:

- The encyclopedia is our index. Not a blog. A map of “what can I do with AI on a Sunday in Kolkata.”
- The website must answer the query before the WhatsApp admin does.
- Tools should default to what people already have (Google account, phone, browser). Paid Claude is a power-up, not a door fee.
- A skill that is used on Tuesday is worth 100× a skill that is applauded on Sunday.

Google wins on **clarity + default + daily use**. Our Sunday must install a weekday habit.

### IKEA / Apple Store (the mega store)

You walk in. You know what the company is. You can buy the thing on the table *today*. You can also see the whole kitchen.

| Store rule | Our rule |
|---|---|
| One hero product on the table | This month’s workshop, date and artifact, above the fold |
| Aisle directory | The Path (Talk → Make → Sell → System → Lead) |
| Showroom rooms | Archive of #1 website, #2 reel, #3 second you |
| Staff who make it | Named hosts, named table captains |
| You leave with a bag | You leave with the file, the Gem, the reel — not a promise |
| Family card | WhatsApp community, earned by showing up, not by applying |
| Next visit is obvious | “October is WhatsApp selling” is on the wall before you exit |

We do **not** make people apply to belong. That was the membership-lander mistake: friction before taste. Sephora does not interview you at the door. They let you try the lipstick. Belonging is a byproduct of building in public in the same room.

We also do **not** hide the catalog. That was the ticket-booth mistake: you cannot love a store that only has one item and no memory.

## 4. What we actually sell

Three shelves. One door.

### Shelf A — The Sunday SKU (cash, proof, flywheel)

A single use case. A finished artifact. Community price. Offline only.

This is the lipstick on the table. It funds the lights. It creates the photos. It is **not** the business.

### Shelf B — The Path (the reason to return)

A 12-month catalog of skills, from “how to talk to a model” to “how to charge for it.” Visible on the site. You can enter at any Sunday. You cannot skip the artifact.

This is why someone comes back in October after they came in September.

### Shelf C — The Room (the actual company)

The people. The WhatsApp. The table captains. The rule: *zero follow-through is the only disqualification.*

This is what Meta would value at a billion dollars and what a course-seller would throw away. We do not throw it away. We also do not charge a membership fee until the room is so good people ask to pay. That is not this quarter.

**Pricing stance:** community price, not cheapness-as-brand. ₹599–₹999 is a serious meal, not a free seminar. Surplus goes back into the room (venue, mics, printed kits, table-captain chai). We are allowed to be a store. We are not allowed to become an ashram that cannot pay the hall.

## 5. Who it is for (and the honest tension)

The velvet rope stays. The audience widens.

**You belong here if**

- You will sit in Salt Lake on a Sunday and do the work with your own hands.
- You want AI to move money, time, or skill — not to decorate a LinkedIn bio.
- You can start from zero. Shop owner, student, CA, tuition teacher, job-seeker, freelancer: the room holds all of them if they *ship*.

**You do not belong here if**

- You want a webinar from bed.
- You collect certificates.
- You want the hype, not the two or three hours of actually doing it.

The tension the old copy named is still the design: *zero tech background is fine. Zero follow-through isn’t.*

What changes at 100 people: we stop pretending only “business owners” exist. Kolkata’s serious beginners include students who will freelance, professionals who will keep their job and get their evenings back, and owners who will fire a vendor. One use case must serve all three **without tracks.** Tracks at 100 people is chaos.

## 6. The 27 September decision

See [WORKSHOP-03-MAKE-A-SECOND-YOU.md](./WORKSHOP-03-MAKE-A-SECOND-YOU.md) for the runbook.

**The SKU:** *The Magic of AI* (50 people). One trick: talk for a few minutes, walk out with a week of finished work plus a saved recipe (a Gemini Gem by default). The working title was “Make a second you.” The founder chose the magic framing and 50 seats. The artifact did not become a sampler.

Why this, and not another website, not another reel, not “AI basics,” not “get a client this week”:

| Filter | Pass / fail |
|---|---|
| Stands alone if you missed #1 and #2 | Pass |
| Works for shop owner *and* student *and* job-seeker | Pass — the “second you” is whoever they are |
| 100 people, one projector, phones + laptops | Pass — Google account, browser, no installs |
| Named artifact you can screenshot tonight | Pass — a Gem with their name on it |
| Daily habit (Google’s test) | Pass — they open it on Tuesday |
| Monetizable without guaranteeing income | Pass — “I will build this for your shop” is a ₹2k–₹10k service |
| Does not repeat June or August | Pass |
| Teaches the meta-skill of the whole Path | Pass — talking to AI until it becomes *theirs* |

Rejected alternatives (kept as later SKUs, not as #3):

- **WhatsApp client machine** — more immediately “money,” worse artifact, more chaos at 100, October’s chapter (Sell).
- **7 videos from one recording** — too soon after #2, account costs, bandwidth death at 100.
- **Build a website (again)** — already shipped in June. Showroom item, not new SKU.
- **“Intro to ChatGPT”** — no artifact. Does not turn the flywheel. Insults the room.

## 7. Format: the 100-person assembly, then small rooms

27 September is not “we grew the cap from 25 to 100 and hope the intimacy survives.”

It is a **founding assembly**:

- One hall. One use case. One projector.
- Tables of 8–10.
- Alumni from #1 and #2 as **table captains** (the Become-a-Host product, finally given a job).
- 3 hours, not 2 (11:00–14:15).
- Then October returns to a 25–40 person room for depth.

This is how churches, TEDx, and every real community actually scale: **a festival, then a parish.** If we try to make every month a festival we will burn the hosts and cheapen the room. If we never throw a festival we will stay a secret.

---

## 8. What we will not do

- We will not go online “just this once.” Offline is the product.
- We will not sell a 12-week recorded course. That is a different company.
- We will not make people apply to belong.
- We will not promise a salary, a client, or AGI.
- We will not run 4 parallel tracks in one Sunday.
- We will not require a paid Claude/ChatGPT subscription as the door fee. Gemini (free, Google account) is the default. Claude is the power-up from #2.
- We will not hide the Path so the homepage can look like an ad.

---

## 9. Success in 90 days (27 Sept → 27 Dec)

Not vanity. Store metrics.

1. **100+ in the hall on 27 Sept**, with 80%+ walking out with a named, working Gem.
2. **Website a stranger understands in 60 seconds** — what this is, what you leave with, what the year looks like, who is in the room.
3. **The Path is public** — five chapters on the site, archive of #1 and #2, October SKU named before people leave in September.
4. **10 table captains** trained and named.
5. **Weekday pulse** — a WhatsApp prompt each Tuesday: “ask your second you to do X, post the result.” Monthly event becomes daily graph.

If those five happen, the company is real. If we only sell 25 more tickets with prettier copy, we ran a class.
