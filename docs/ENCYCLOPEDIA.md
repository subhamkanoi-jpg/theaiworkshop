## 0. What this place is

The AI Workshop is Kolkata’s offline store for applied AI.

You come on a Sunday. You build one real thing with your own hands. You leave with the file, the video, or the assistant — not with notes about a file, a video, or an assistant. You can start from zero. You cannot start from “I’ll watch.”

There is a Path. You do not have to walk it in order. Every Sunday stands alone. The Path is why the year is not a pile of random classes.

There is a Room. It is in Salt Lake. The same faces come back. WhatsApp is the corridor between Sundays.

**The law of the room:** zero tech background is fine. Zero follow-through is not.

## 1. The Path

Five chapters. Every workshop on the calendar belongs to one of them.

```
TALK  →  MAKE  →  SELL  →  SYSTEM  →  LEAD
  ↑         ↑        ↑         ↑         ↑
 how you    the      how the   how it    how the
 brief a    artifact money     runs on   room
 model      itself   appears   Tuesday   grows
```

### Chapter 1 — Talk

The meta-skill. If you cannot brief a model, every other Sunday is theatre.

**You are done with Talk when you can:**

- Give an AI a job in one paragraph: who you are, who it’s for, what “good” looks like, what to never do.
- Paste *examples* of your own work instead of adjectives (“make it professional”).
- Save the brief that worked, and reuse it, instead of starting from a blank chat every time.
- Notice when the model is guessing and feed it a fact.

**Sunday shape:** not a lecture on “what is a token.” A live rewriting of bad prompts into briefs that produce usable work.

**Default tools:** Gemini (free), Claude, ChatGPT — the brief is portable. We do not marry a logo.

### Chapter 2 — Make

The showroom. This is what people can see you did.

**Shipped**

| # | Date | Artifact | Tool default |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 28 June 2026 | A live one-page website | AI website builders + your copy |
| 2 | 30 August 2026 | A subtitled reel from raw phone footage | Claude + FFmpeg + Whisper |

**Still on the Make shelf**

- A 30-day content set (captions + image prompts + calendar) in one afternoon.
- A brand kit: name, colours, on-brand photo style, three templates.
- Documents that used to take a Sunday: proposals, menus, price lists, reports.

**You are done with a Make Sunday when the artifact is exported, on your phone, and you could do the next one without us.**

### Chapter 3 — Sell

The reason “useful” becomes “livelihood.”

**On the Sell shelf (starting October)**

- WhatsApp that brings customers: catalogues, replies, follow-ups, a broadcast that does not sound like a broadcast.
- An offer, written and priced, that a stranger understands.
- Outreach that is specific (one person, one problem, one next step) instead of “Dear Sir I am an AI expert.”

**The honest line:** we teach you to make a thing someone can pay for. We do not owe you the first rupee. The rupee is your Tuesday.

### Chapter 4 — System

The Google test: do they open it on a Tuesday?

The weekday habit is the System chapter. Workshop #3 (27 September) is **The Magic of AI** — the Talk chapter's public SKU — and it still saves the recipe so Tuesday works. See the runbook.

**You are done with System when:**

- You have a named assistant that already knows your work.
- You used it on three real tasks from your actual week, in the room.
- You have a weekly rhythm written down: when you talk to it, what you bring it, where the output goes.
- You could build a smaller version of the same thing for someone else.

Without System, Make is a hobby and Sell is a mood.

### Chapter 5 — Lead

How a 60-person WhatsApp group becomes a 400-person city without the three hosts dying.

**Lead looks like**

- Table captain: you have shipped at least one Sunday, you run a table of 8–10, you do not lecture, you unblock.
- Host: you teach *your* use case on a later Sunday, the way Become-a-Host always wanted.
- Librarian: you add a recipe to this encyclopedia that a beginner can follow.

You cannot skip to Lead. The room can smell it.

## 2. How a Sunday works

Same ritual every month, so the store feels like a store.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| −30 min | Doors. Chai. Wifi. Account check. Table captains on the floor. |
| 0:00 | Why this artifact. One finished example on the big screen. |
| 0:20 | You gather *your* raw material (copy, footage, facts, chats). |
| 0:40 | Build, following the projector. Captains unblock, they do not take the laptop. |
| ~2:00 | Break. |
| After break | You make the artifact *yours* — three real tasks, not the sample. |
| Last 30 | How this can be charged for. 6–8 people show their thing. Group photo. Next Sunday named. WhatsApp. |

**You bring:** phone, charger, laptop if you have one, a Google account, raw material for *your* life (optional — we always have samples).

**You do not need:** a computer science degree, Premiere, VS Code, a paid AI plan (unless a specific Sunday says so, in which case we say so a week ahead).

**Wifi dies:** the brief is on paper / PDF. The thinking still happens in the room. The click-together becomes homework with captains on WhatsApp. The Sunday is not cancelled because Jio had a feeling.

## 4. The one skill underneath every Sunday

Everything in this encyclopedia is a version of this loop:

1. **Job** — one sentence. “Write 7 WhatsApp follow-ups for a Salt Lake dental clinic that feels human.”
2. **Context** — who you are, who it’s for, facts, constraints, language (English / Hindi / Bangla).
3. **Examples** — 2–3 pieces of work you already like (your own or stolen with a note).
4. **Tests** — “Give me 3 options. Flag anything you invented.”
5. **Save** — the brief that worked becomes the Gem / Project / GPT / a note you reuse.

If you only remember one page of this encyclopedia, remember this one.

Bad: *“Make this professional and engaging.”*
Good: *“You are writing as Riya, who runs a 12-table vegetarian restaurant in Sector V. Audience: office people within 1 km, 1–2 minutes of reading, Bangla or simple English. Never use the word ‘gastronomy.’ Here are 3 captions that already got replies: [paste]. Write 7 more for next week’s thali. Mark any fact you had to invent.”*

That paragraph *is* the workshop, wearing different clothes each month.

## 5. Who walks in (and what they walk out with)

The Sunday is the same. The *content they paste* is theirs.

| Person | What they paste into the brief | What “monetize” means later |
|---|---|---|
| Shop / clinic / restaurant owner | Price list, WhatsApp chats, photos, how they currently reply | Hours back; maybe fire a vendor; maybe a Gem for the counter staff |
| Freelancer / agency of one | Old proposals, captions, client complaints | Faster delivery; a packaged service (“I’ll build you a second you”) |
| Job-seeker | CV, job description, emails they are scared to send | Better applications this week; not a miracle placement |
| Student | Assignment briefs, internship messages, project explanations | A skill they can sell to a local business as a mini-service |
| Professional keeping the job | Emails, reports, the weekly task they hate | Evenings back. Quietly. |

No tracks. No “business owners to the left, students to the right.” Tracks at 100 people is how you lose an hour.

## 7. Community operating system

**WhatsApp is not the product.** It is the corridor.

Rules that keep it a room, not a dump:

1. You get the link after you have a seat, not as a marketing channel that anyone can lurk in forever.
2. The only spam that is allowed is shipping: “here is what my second you wrote for the clinic.”
3. A Tuesday pulse, once a week: one job to give your assistant, one screenshot if you want.
4. Questions get answered by captains first, hosts second. That is how Lead is trained.
5. No crypto, no “my AI tool,” no hiring pitches without a host saying so.

**Belonging:** you showed up, you built, you are in. No application form. The form can come back years later if the room is overfull. It is not how you start a city.

**Hosts:** Yogesh, Neeraj, Subham — named on the site, with a sentence each about why they are in the room. Anonymous avatars were a waste of three real people.

## 10. What “monetize” means, without lying

People can honestly make money from this Path in three ways, in this order:

1. **Keep money** — stop paying ₹3,000 a reel, ₹15,000 a website, ₹5,000 a month to a “social media person” who posts nothing. This is the common case. It is already a win.
2. **Sell a skill** — “I will make your shop a second you / a month of captions / a one-page site.” First customers are people they already know. First fee is ₹2,000–₹10,000, not ₹1 lakh.
3. **Get hired or get a client** — better CV, better outreach, better proof (URLs and reels beat adjectives). No Sunday can promise a job.

We teach 1 as the default, 2 as the unlocked door, 3 as a possible side effect. Anyone advertising 3 as a guarantee is running a different, worse, company.
